A manifold is a space that locally resembles the Euclidean space. Precisely, a manifold $\mathbb{M}$ of dimension $n$ is a space of dimension $n$ where each point $p \in \mathbb{M}$ has a neighborhood that is homeomorphic to an open subset of $n$-dimensional Euclidean space.

> [!NOTE] Homeomorphism
> Is a [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective "Bijective") and [continuous function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function#Continuous_functions_between_topological_spaces "Continuous function") between [topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_space "Topological space") that has a continuous [inverse function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_function "Inverse function").

Informally, it can contain lines and circles but not **self-crossing curves**.
