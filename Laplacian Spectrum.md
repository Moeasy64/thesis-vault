The Laplacian Spectrum $LS(G) = \{\lambda_1\ \leq \lambda_2 \leq \dots \leq \lambda_{|V|}\}$ of a graph $G=(V,E)$ is the *set of eigenvalues ordered by crescent* of the [[Laplacian Matrix]] of $G$.

This gives us some useful information about the structure of the graph, such as the **Algebraic Connectivity** or **Fiedler Value**:  $\lambda_2$ is the secondo eigenvalue in the Laplacian Spectrum and it is called Fiedler Value. If $\lambda_2 > 0$ then $G$ is a fully connected graph.

*corollary 1:* the number of eigenvalues $\lambda_i = 0$ reflects the number of connected components inside the graph

*corollary 2:* $\lambda_1$ is always zero.
